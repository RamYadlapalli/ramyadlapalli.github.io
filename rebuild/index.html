Moving a live logistics platform from an on premises monolith to Azure

Ram Yadlapalli · November 2026

In 2018 I led the rebuild of our first and final mile optimization and execution platform for LTL carriers. We moved it off our own servers and onto Azure, and broke up the ASP.NET monolith we had started with. Carriers kept running on it the whole time.

I was the key architect and lead developer on the web app and the backend services, and on the React Native driver app as well.

I was one of the first two engineers, and in 2017 we built the MVP as a single ASP.NET application running on premises. After a year with real carriers on it we understood much better how they used it, and it was clear that some parts needed to scale and change on their own. Keeping everything in one deployable unit had started to hold us back.

Drivers ran routes on the platform every day, so we could not take it offline to cut over. The platform is also multi tenant, and every step had to keep each carrier's data separate.

How we split it

We split services based on how carriers actually used the system, one piece at a time. New services were written in Node.js and TypeScript, with C# and .NET where that fit better, and they run on Kubernetes (AKS) with data in Azure SQL.

The platform before and after the rebuild.

Where work can be queued, services hand it to each other through AMQP messaging and Azure Service Bus, which lets each one scale separately. Driver events and GPS pings are our busiest traffic, so they come in through Event Hubs. Photos and integration files go to Blob and File Storage instead of the database. SignalR and the other Azure pieces came in the same way, once we had a real need for them.

The optimization engine belongs to the operations research team, which reported to me. I worked on the solution design with them and built the services and integrations around the engine.

Where it is now

Carriers stayed live through the whole rebuild. The same architecture now runs at 11 LTL carriers in the US and Canada, for about 20,000 drivers and 1,000 planners and dispatchers, at roughly a million transactions an hour and 99.7% uptime. We moved to monthly releases on it in 2022.

I still go back to it, mostly to look at cloud cost. For each component I ask what I would change if I were building it today, and we refactor when the answer is worth the effort.
