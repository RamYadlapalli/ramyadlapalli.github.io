Integrating with AS400 and custom TMS systems without asking carriers to build much

Ram Yadlapalli · Case study · Engineering

Every carrier we deploy has a system of record, usually an AS400 or a custom TMS, and an IT team with its own priorities. I designed a small set of integration patterns so we can connect to whatever the carrier has, and so the carrier has as little as possible to build.

At a glance

  • My role: led the technical discovery and designed the integrations for every carrier deployment since 2018
  • Carrier systems: AS400 and custom TMS
  • Patterns: streaming, APIs, FTP triggered flows, and event triggered Azure Logic Apps

The problem

A deployment takes 1 to 6 months, and the hard part is usually not our platform. It is getting data in and out of the carrier's system. Shipment data comes from their side, and plans and status updates need to go back. Some carriers have modern APIs. Many run on an AS400 or a TMS built in house years ago.

The constraints

  • Carrier IT teams are small and busy, so anything they have to build slows the deployment down.
  • Their systems can't change much, and some can only exchange files.

The design decisions

Instead of building a new integration for each carrier, I designed a few patterns and pick the one that fits during discovery.

Four ways data moves between the platform and a carrier's system, picked per carrier during discovery.

PatternHow it worksWhen it fits
StreamingEvents flow continuously in both directions.The carrier can publish and consume events.
APIsOur APIs and theirs, called on demand or when something changes.The carrier has APIs, or can build a few.
FTP triggered flowsA file landing on FTP triggers a flow that loads it, and we drop files back the same way.The system can only exchange files, which is common on an AS400.
No client side integrationAn event on our side triggers an Azure Logic App that uploads files and writes data directly into the carrier's system.The carrier's IT team has no capacity to build anything.

With the last pattern, the carrier has nothing to build or maintain on their side.

The result

I have led every carrier deployment since 2018, and the integration is designed so the carrier's side of the work stays small. Carriers ended up with fewer routes, fewer miles, nearly zero missed appointments, and 75% less planning time, and moved to centralized planning and dispatching.
